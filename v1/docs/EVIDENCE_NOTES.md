# Evidence Notes

## Executive evidence map

### Original Classcraft case-study numbers

**East Paulding Middle School — 85% drop in referrals in one term.** This figure appears in Classcraft/HMH historical marketing/case-study material. It is a concrete implementation example, not a randomized controlled causal estimate.

**Tabor City Middle School — 91% drop in classroom disruptions.** The vendor case study reports classroom disruptions falling from 59 to 5; referrals from 257 to 49 (80.9%); fights from 14 to 2; not-following-directions incidents from 31 to 3; bus incidents from 21 to 5; disrespect-to-staff incidents from 36 to 5. These are descriptive school-level pre/post data and may be affected by implementation, reporting, time-period, staffing, policy, and contextual changes.

### Classcraft-specific / Classcraft-centered research

A 2021 content analysis/meta-analysis focused on Classcraft and related gamification learning experiences reported learning-achievement effect `d = 0.621` (95% CI 0.413–0.829) and motivation effect `d = 0.608` (95% CI 0.415–0.801). The paper should not be summarized as a clean Classcraft-only RCT estimate.

The U.S. Institute of Education Sciences funded SRI International for a five-year initial efficacy study beginning in 2023. The IES project description explicitly states that Classcraft had not been rigorously evaluated in a randomized controlled trial and identifies the study as filling that gap. The grant page also states that Classcraft was used by 30,000+ teachers and 1 million active student participants worldwide at the time.

### Broader gamification evidence

Sailer & Homner's 2020 meta-analysis reported significant effects on:

- cognitive learning outcomes: `g = 0.49` (95% CI 0.30–0.69)
- motivational learning outcomes: `g = 0.36` (95% CI 0.18–0.54)
- behavioral learning outcomes: `g = 0.25` (95% CI 0.04–0.46)

Bai, Hew & Huang's 2020 meta-analysis included 30 independent interventions / 3,202 participants and reported an overall academic-performance effect `g = 0.504` (95% CI 0.284–0.723). Their qualitative synthesis found enthusiasm, feedback, recognition, and goal setting as reasons learners enjoyed gamification, while lack of added utility and anxiety/jealousy were reasons for dislike.

A later meta-analysis examining intrinsic motivation and self-determination constructs reported overall intrinsic-motivation `g = 0.257`, autonomy `g = 0.638`, relatedness `g = 1.776`, and a smaller competence effect. These values should be interpreted with the study's heterogeneity and design differences in mind.

### PBIS evidence

A group-randomized effectiveness trial included 12,344 elementary students across 37 schools and followed outcomes over four school years. Children in SWPBIS schools were 33% less likely to receive an office discipline referral than students in comparison schools, and the study reported significant effects on several behavioral/social-emotional measures.

The 2024 What Works Clearinghouse practice guide gives strong-evidence recommendations for co-establishing/modeling/teaching clear behavior expectations, reminding students of expected behavior, and acknowledging expected behavior through positive attention, praise, and rewards.

## Evidence hierarchy used in the deck

- `strong / RCT`: randomized or high-quality causal evidence.
- `independent meta-analysis`: synthesis across multiple studies; quality depends on included designs and heterogeneity.
- `ongoing RCT`: important but results not yet assumed.
- `vendor case study`: implementation illustration, hypothesis generation, and feasibility signal; not equivalent to causal evidence.
- `design proposal`: architectural or experience concept requiring local evaluation.
