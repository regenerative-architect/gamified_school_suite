# High-Impact Launch — Run of Show

## T−21 to T−8 days: curiosity without coercion
Deploy changing symbols, constellation fragments, sealed display cases, QR-free clue cards, teacher easter eggs, morning-announcement fragments, and a neutral message such as **“Something new is assembling.”** Do not tie the reveal to perfect behavior or threaten cancellation.

## T−7 to T−1: community readiness
Reveal the readiness meter. A day advances when schoolwide operational criteria are met: staff preparation, safe routines, launch equipment tests, accessibility checks, and representative student feedback. Positive school-climate indicators can be included, but no named student can cost the school a day.

## Launch morning
- Exterior/entry signage changes before students arrive.
- Hallway wayfinding becomes a “map.”
- Optional ambient projection/audio begins before first bell at an accessible volume.
- Staff use consistent language and know the non-gamified alternative.
- Safety routes, exits, supervision, and accessibility cues remain visually dominant.

## Assembly / reveal (25–40 min)
1. **Cold open (2 min):** lights/projection/narration; no strobing.
2. **The premise (4 min):** “school is becoming a cooperative learning world,” not “obedience earns fun.”
3. **Live quest (7 min):** whole-room puzzle or knowledge challenge with multiple ways to contribute.
4. **Role reveal (5 min):** builder, researcher, mentor, explorer, creator, connector—roles describe contribution styles, not fixed identities.
5. **World map + progression (5 min):** demonstrate quests, mastery, community milestones, and unlockable *optional* content.
6. **Safeguards (3 min):** grades, essential learning, accommodations, and dignity are not game stakes.
7. **First mission (5–10 min):** achievable, cooperative, curriculum-linked task completed before students leave.

## Optional immersive layer
Projection mapping, AR markers, student-created audio, large-format displays, responsive lighting, or a mascot performance can amplify impact. Run a sensory/accessibility review first and provide an equivalent low-stimulation experience.

## First 72 hours
Keep mechanics simple. Collect friction reports. Celebrate effort and cooperation more than raw point totals. Fix confusing rules immediately. Do not introduce a store, competitive season, or complex economy until students understand the learning loop.
