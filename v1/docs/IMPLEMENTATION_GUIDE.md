# Implementation Guide

## 1. Form the launch team

Minimum roles: school leader/sponsor, teacher lead, student-advisory facilitator, safeguarding lead, accessibility/special-education representative, data/evaluation owner, IT/privacy owner, and communications/family liaison.

## 2. Define the non-negotiables

Write 3–5 positively stated expectations. Define what can earn recognition, what can never be traded for points, how errors are corrected, which records are private, how accommodations work, who can change rules, and how a student can challenge a record.

## 3. Baseline first

Collect at least several weeks of existing measures where feasible: referrals, attendance, mastery/assessment, student belonging/engagement pulse, teacher workload, and implementation context. Avoid creating a new surveillance apparatus simply to populate a dashboard.

## 4. Launch narrow

Start with:

- six flexible contribution roles;
- mastery XP and community XP;
- three to five reward privileges;
- two or three recurring quest templates;
- one community milestone;
- the readiness/signage campaign;
- one staff dashboard.

Do not launch every mechanic at once.

## 5. Anticipation campaign

Ten to fourteen days before launch, introduce symbols and mystery language. Seven days before launch, begin the community-readiness streak. Do not display student names, negative counts, or “who cost us a day.”

## 6. Reveal day

A recommended sequence:

1. Music/light/projection cue.
2. Five-minute story trailer or live performance.
3. Explain the real purpose in plain language.
4. Students choose contribution roles.
5. Teams solve one cooperative challenge.
6. Award the first collective milestone.
7. Open the first academic quest.
8. End with a concrete “what happens tomorrow.”

Optional AR/projection mapping can increase novelty, but a low-tech version should preserve the exact same game loop.

## 7. First month

Week 1: reward clarity and feedback speed.

Week 2: add student-authored random events and optional side quests.

Week 3: introduce evidence-backed mastery badges and mentoring.

Week 4: inspect equity, workload, novelty decay, anxiety, metric gaming, and participation by student subgroup/context. Simplify before adding more.

## 8. Pilot evaluation

Predefine outcomes and guardrails. A credible pilot can conclude that the program did not work well enough. Compare with baseline and, where feasible, a matched comparison grade/class/school. Combine administrative data with student and staff voice.

## 9. Scale

Scale classroom → grade → school → district only after implementation fidelity and workload are stable. Centralize schemas, safeguarding rules, accessibility, evidence standards, and reporting; allow local narrative, role names, quest themes, and culturally relevant rewards.
