# Family FAQ

## Is this replacing teaching with a video game?
No. The game layer organizes existing learning, feedback, collaboration, and progress. Teachers remain responsible for instruction and assessment.

## Are grades based on XP?
They should not be. XP is a feedback/progress signal unless a school explicitly maps a mechanic to an academically valid assessment rule. The prototype keeps academic mastery separate from game currency.

## Can my child be publicly ranked for behavior?
The recommended design prohibits public discipline leaderboards and public shaming. Individual support information belongs in protected school processes.

## What happens if there is a fight or other serious incident during the launch countdown?
The incident is handled through normal safety, safeguarding, restorative, and disciplinary procedures. A single student's incident does **not** publicly reset the whole school's countdown. Staff may privately delay an event if schoolwide safety or operational readiness requires it.

## Can students participate without animations/fantasy themes?
Yes. The system includes reduced-motion and low-gamification approaches, and schools should provide equivalent access for students whose preferences, disabilities, cultures, or beliefs make a particular presentation unsuitable.

## What data is collected?
The prototype works without student PII. A production deployment should publish a data inventory, retention schedule, access rules, vendor list, incident-response plan, and family-facing privacy notice before collecting identifiable student information.

## Does AI make discipline or grading decisions?
Not in the recommended architecture. AI can help draft quests, summarize public evidence, or suggest alternatives, but consequential decisions require authorized human review and applicable school policy.
