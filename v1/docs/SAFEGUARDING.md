# Safeguarding & Anti-Inversion Design

## Why the readiness countdown is not an incident-free contest

A school may want to say “big changes unlock after X good days.” The safe version measures **community readiness**, not whether every child avoided every incident. A single student should not be made responsible for hundreds of peers losing a promised experience.

Possible schoolwide readiness criteria:

- core supervision posts were staffed;
- transition routines were taught and used;
- staff completed launch/safeguarding preparation;
- aggregate environment was safe enough for the planned event;
- accessibility accommodations were ready;
- no unresolved schoolwide operational hazard made the event unsafe.

Individual incidents are handled privately under existing school policy, safeguarding obligations, restorative/support processes, and due process. They are not announced as the reason “everyone lost the game.”

## Prohibited or high-risk mechanics

- public negative leaderboards;
- collective punishment;
- peer pressure to conceal incidents;
- loss of food, bathroom access, core instruction, disability supports, counseling, or safeguarding access;
- AI-generated diagnosis, threat scoring, or disciplinary decisions;
- pay-to-win or family-spending advantages;
- hidden surveillance or telemetry;
- permanent streak loss after unavoidable absence;
- irreversible labels such as “bad,” “low-level,” or “problem player.”

## Safer replacements

- private mastery views;
- cooperative team milestones;
- forgiving streaks and recovery tokens;
- specific praise rather than generic point inflation;
- evidence-backed badges;
- restorative repair quests when appropriate;
- student-authored quests and random events;
- multiple equivalent pathways for students who dislike fantasy/game aesthetics;
- human review and appeal for consequential records.

## Student data

The prototype does not require student PII. Production deployments should minimize data, define purpose and retention, segregate support/safeguarding records from game data, use least-privilege roles, encrypt data in transit and at rest where applicable, and comply with relevant local privacy/education law and district policy.
