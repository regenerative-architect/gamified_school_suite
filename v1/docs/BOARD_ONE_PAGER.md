# Level Up Learning — Board / Superintendent One-Pager

## Decision requested
Authorize a **90-day, evaluation-first pilot** of a configurable K–12 gamified learning and school-climate layer. The pilot does not replace the SIS/LMS, discipline code, safeguarding procedures, special-education plans, or professional judgment.

## Why test it
Independent gamification meta-analyses report small-to-moderate positive average effects on learning, motivation, and behavior, while implementation quality and context materially affect results. Classcraft's published school case studies report large reductions in referrals/disruptions, but those figures are vendor case studies rather than randomized causal estimates. The pilot therefore treats impact as a hypothesis to test locally, not a promised outcome.

## What changes for students
- Curriculum work can be represented as quests, skill trees, cooperative missions, collections, and mastery pathways.
- Recognition emphasizes growth, contribution, teamwork, repair, and demonstrated learning—not popularity or public discipline rankings.
- Essential learning is never locked behind game progress.
- A student can use a low-gamification presentation while retaining equivalent access and credit.

## Launch approach
A teaser campaign creates curiosity without threatening students: evolving symbols, sealed displays, clue cards, progress constellations, and a visible **community readiness streak**. Readiness reflects schoolwide preparation and positive routines. One student's incident never resets a public countdown. Serious incidents remain confidential and use normal safeguarding/restorative processes.

## Safeguards
No public behavior leaderboards; no pay-to-win; no loss of grades or essential services as a game penalty; no collective punishment; no student PII required by the prototype; role-based permissions; review/appeal path; accessibility and reduced-motion modes; exportable data; explicit evidence labels; human review for AI-assisted content.

## Pilot outcomes
Use pre-specified measures: attendance, office discipline referrals, classroom disruption sampling, assignment completion, belonging/safety survey items, teacher workload, family sentiment, accessibility incidents, and subgroup checks. Compare baseline and pilot periods cautiously; do not attribute causality without a suitable design.

## Gate for scale
Scale only if the pilot meets locally chosen thresholds for safety, equity, educational benefit, teacher burden, privacy, and technical reliability. Preserve a rollback path.
